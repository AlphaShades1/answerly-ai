Best Canvas Quiz Solver in 2026: Answerly AI vs the Alternatives

Comparison · Updated September 2026 · ~8 min read

Search "Canvas quiz solver" and you'll find a dozen options: AI chatbots, answer-library sites, and a pile of browser extensions that promise instant answers. Most of them do something — but on a real, timed Canvas quiz, the differences matter a lot. Here's an honest side-by-side of the main choices in 2026, and why Answerly AI ends up being the better pick for most students.

What actually makes a Canvas quiz solver good

Before the comparison, it's worth naming the criteria that separate a genuinely useful tool from one that just looks good in a screenshot:

  • Works inside Canvas — you shouldn't have to copy every question out to another tab.
  • Question-type coverage — multiple choice is easy; matching, fill-in-the-blank, and free response are where most tools fall over.
  • Speed on a timed quiz — an answer that takes three minutes to arrive is useless when the clock is running.
  • Explains the reasoning — a bare letter teaches you nothing; the "why" is what helps on the next question and the exam.
  • Discretion & privacy — a clean, controllable interface that isn't screaming for attention.

1. Generic AI chatbots (ChatGPT, Gemini, etc.)

Best for: open-ended writing and one-off questions

A general AI chatbot is genuinely smart, and for essay help or explaining a concept it's excellent. The problem is workflow. On a Canvas quiz you have to select each question, copy it, switch tabs, paste it, wait, read the answer, switch back, and click — for every single question. On a ten-question timed quiz that's a lot of tab-juggling, and it's easy to miscopy a question or lose the answer choices. It's a powerful brain with no connection to the page you're actually working on.

2. Answer-library sites (Chegg, Course Hero)

Best for: common textbook problems that others have already posted

These services rely on a library of previously answered questions. When your exact question is in the database, you get a worked solution — great. When it isn't (a new question, a reworded one, or a question with shuffled answer order), you're stuck posting it and waiting, sometimes minutes, sometimes longer. They also live on a separate website, so nothing happens where your quiz actually is, and they don't touch matching or fill-in-the-blank questions that only exist inside your Canvas course.

3. Generic Canvas "answer-bot" extensions

Best for: pasting a raw answer with no explanation

There's a whole category of browser extensions that inject an answer into a Canvas quiz. Some are fast — but they tend to share the same weaknesses: they only handle basic multiple choice, they break the moment Canvas updates its markup, they give you a letter with no reasoning, and they offer no control over how visible they are on screen. They're built to dump answers, not to help you actually understand the material or stay in control of your workflow.

4. Answerly AI

Best for: answering any Canvas question fast — and understanding it

Answerly AI is built specifically for the thing everyone else does awkwardly: solving questions inside Canvas. It's a Chrome extension that runs on the quiz page itself, so there's no copying, no tab-switching, no separate website. Here's where it pulls ahead:

  • Real-time AI, not a library. It generates the answer on the spot, so a brand-new or reworded question is no problem — nothing needs to have been "posted" before.
  • Stealth mode & Privacy Guard. A clean, controllable interface with a discreet mode, plus Privacy Guard to keep the experience distraction-free while you work.
  • Fast. Answers arrive in seconds, right where the question is — which is the whole point when a quiz is timed.

So which should you pick?

If you mostly write essays, a general AI chatbot is a fine companion. If you're chasing one common textbook problem, an answer library might have it. But for the everyday reality of getting through Canvas quizzes quickly and actually learning from them, Answerly AI is the better pick — it does the in-Canvas job the others weren't built for, and it does it in one click.
