How to Study for a Canvas Exam When You Are Out of Time

Study guide · Updated October 2026 · ~7 min read

Most study advice assumes you have two weeks. This assumes you have one evening, which is the situation people are actually in when they go looking for help. The goal is not to learn the whole course. It is to convert the hours you have into the largest possible number of marks.

Start by finding out what the exam actually is

Before opening a single note, open the assignment page. You want four things: how many questions, how many points, whether there is a time limit, and how many attempts you get. Canvas shows all of this before you begin.

These change the plan completely. A 50-question multiple-choice quiz with unlimited attempts is a different problem from a 10-question timed exam with one attempt. People routinely burn an evening preparing for the wrong format.

Work backwards from the points

Not all topics are worth the same. If the exam covers six chapters and your instructor called two of them "emphasised", those two are probably half the marks. Study in descending order of expected points per hour, not in chapter order.

If you have an old quiz, a practice quiz or a study guide, that is your single best signal. Instructors reuse structure heavily. The practice quiz is rarely the same questions, but it is very often the same shape.

Use retrieval, not rereading

Rereading notes feels productive and is close to worthless. The effect is well documented: recognition is not recall, and exams test recall. Close the notes and try to produce the answer from memory, then check. Getting it wrong and correcting it is what builds the memory.

The cheapest version: turn each heading in your notes into a question, then answer it out loud without looking. Anything you stumble on goes on a short list, and that list is your revision material for the final hour.

Three hours, realistically allocated

  • First 20 minutes — read the exam's structure, skim the study guide, write down the 10 topics most likely to appear.
  • Next 90 minutes — retrieval practice on those 10 topics, hardest first, keeping a running list of what you miss.
  • Next 40 minutes — work real practice questions if you have them. If not, write questions yourself and answer them.
  • Final 30 minutes — only the missed list. Nothing new.

During the exam

Answer everything you know first and leave the rest. On most Canvas quizzes you can move freely between questions, so there is no reason to sit on question three while twelve easy marks wait at the end. Check whether backtracking is enabled before relying on this, because New Quizzes lets instructors turn it off.

Never leave a multiple-choice question blank unless there is a penalty for wrong answers, which is rare. Watch multiple-answer questions especially: most are graded all or nothing, so a partially correct selection usually scores zero.

What to do differently next time

The highest-return habit by a distance is twenty minutes of retrieval practice per course per week, rather than six hours the night before. That is useless advice tonight, which is why it is at the end. But it is the difference between repeating this evening every term and not.

Read the quiz settings first. They are worth ten minutes of study.

Before you open a single slide, open the quiz page itself. Canvas publishes most of the rules up front, and each one changes how you should spend the time you have left.

  • Attempts allowed. If it is more than one, your first attempt is reconnaissance. Nothing changes your strategy more than this, and students routinely miss it.
  • Time limit. Divide it by the question count. Ninety seconds a question is a recall test; six minutes a question means reasoning, and cramming definitions will not save you.
  • One question at a time, and whether you can go back. If backtracking is locked, you cannot skim ahead and triage. You must answer in order, so your first instinct matters far more.
  • Shuffled answers. Memorising "the answer is C" is worthless. Memorise the content.
  • When results are shown. If correct answers appear after the first attempt and you have more than one, the first attempt is the single most efficient study resource available to you.
  • Due versus available-until. These are different dates. Check whether late submissions are accepted at a penalty rather than refused outright.

Work backwards from the points

Not all material is worth the same. If the quiz is forty questions and your instructor said it covers chapters nine through twelve, that is roughly ten questions a chapter. A chapter you already half-know is not where the marks are. The chapter you have never opened is worth more per minute than polishing the one you are comfortable with, right up until you run out of time.

If there is a study guide, treat it as the actual specification rather than a suggestion. Instructors build quizzes from the same outline they hand out, and the overlap is usually far higher than students assume.

Retrieval, not rereading

With limited time the instinct is to reread notes until they feel familiar. Familiarity is not recall, and the gap between them is exactly where exam marks disappear.

Close the notes and write down what you remember on a blank page. It will be uncomfortable and the page will look sparse. That discomfort is the point: the holes you just found are the highest-value minutes you have left. Then open the notes and fill only those holes. Repeat with a shorter list.

This is slower per page and dramatically faster per mark.

Three hours, realistically allocated

  • 0:00–0:15. Read the quiz settings. Find the study guide or the stated chapter range. Write the topic list.
  • 0:15–0:35. Blank-page recall across the whole topic list. Do not look anything up. Mark every topic you could not explain.
  • 0:35–2:00. Work only the marked topics, hardest first while you are freshest. Definitions, the relationships between them, and one worked example each.
  • 2:00–2:30. Second blank-page pass. The list should be visibly shorter. Whatever survives twice gets written on one index card.
  • 2:30–2:50. Practice questions if any exist. If not, invent them from your own topic list — writing a question forces you to decide what the answer actually depends on.
  • 2:50–3:00. Stop studying. Read the index card. Check your connection and that nothing is about to update your browser.

Tactics by question type

  • Multiple choice. Answer before you read the options where you can. Reading the options first lets a well-written distractor overwrite the answer you already knew.
  • Multiple select. Treat each option as its own true or false question. Partial credit usually applies, so a considered half-answer beats an empty one.
  • Matching. Lock in the pairs you are certain of first; each one shrinks the pool for the rest.
  • Fill in the blank. Usually matched against a list of accepted strings. Write the plainest form of the term, singular, no article, no explanation.
  • Numeric. Check the unit and the required precision before you type. Correct value, wrong unit scores zero.

During the exam

If you can move freely between questions, do one fast pass answering only what you know cold. This banks the certain marks before the clock becomes a factor and often jogs loose the answer to something earlier. Then take a second pass at the rest.

If backtracking is locked, that approach is unavailable and you should commit to a reasonable answer rather than burning four minutes on question three while twenty-two remain.

Answer everything. Canvas does not deduct for a wrong answer on a standard quiz, so an empty box is a guaranteed zero where a guess is not.

What to do differently next time

The reason this situation is stressful is not that three hours is too little. It is that the three hours arrived at the end instead of being spread out. Twenty minutes of blank-page recall a week, from the first week, beats any amount of panic the night before — and it takes less total time.

Frequently asked questions

Is it better to sleep or keep studying?

Sleep, in almost every case. Sleep deprivation degrades recall and reasoning more than one extra hour of revision adds, and exams test both.

Do practice quizzes count toward my grade?

Usually not, but check the points value on the assignment page. Some instructors make practice attempts worth a small amount for completion.

How many attempts do I get?

The quiz page shows this before you start. If multiple attempts are allowed, the instructor also chooses whether the highest, latest or average score counts.

Does Canvas penalise me for taking the full time?

No. Time taken is recorded but carries no grade consequence by itself.

Should I answer a question I have no idea about?

Yes. Standard Canvas quizzes do not subtract marks for wrong answers, so a guess strictly beats a blank.

Is it worth doing practice questions if there are only a few?

Yes, and write your own once they run out. Composing a question forces you to work out what the answer depends on, which is the same skill the exam is testing.
