The 7 Best Study Tools for College Students in 2026

Roundup · Updated August 2026 · ~7 min read

The right study tool doesn't just save you time — it changes how well the material sticks. These are the seven that consistently earn their place in a college student's toolkit in 2026, from the classics to the newer AI-powered helpers.

1. Anki

Best for: long-term memorization

Anki is the gold standard for spaced repetition. It shows you a flashcard right before you're about to forget it, which is scientifically the most efficient way to move facts into long-term memory. The interface is dated and the learning curve is real, but for vocabulary, formulas, and dense factual courses, nothing beats it — and it's free.

2. Quizlet

Best for: quick, shareable flashcards

Quizlet is Anki's friendlier cousin. It's faster to set up, looks great, and millions of pre-made study sets already exist for common courses — so you can often skip making cards entirely. Its practice-test and matching-game modes make review feel less like a chore.

3. Notion

Best for: organizing everything in one place

Notion is where a lot of students now keep their entire academic life: class notes, assignment trackers, reading lists, and group-project pages. The flexibility is the point — you build a system that matches how you think, instead of forcing your notes into someone else's template.

4. Answerly AI

Best for: understanding Canvas questions fast

Most study tools help you prepare before a quiz. Answerly AI helps in the moment you're stuck on one. It's a Chrome extension that works right inside Canvas — click any question and it gives you the correct answer and the reasoning behind it, so you learn the concept instead of just moving on. It handles multiple choice, matching, and fill-in-the-blank, and its screenshot tool can read free-response questions too. For students who want to check their understanding and stop wasting an hour Googling one question, it's become a genuinely useful part of the workflow.

5. Grammarly

Best for: essays and written assignments

For any course with writing, Grammarly catches the grammar slips, awkward phrasing, and tone issues you stop seeing after staring at a draft for two hours. The free tier covers the essentials; the paid tier adds clarity and vocabulary suggestions that noticeably tighten an essay.

6. Wolfram Alpha

Best for: STEM problem-solving

For math, physics, chemistry, and engineering, Wolfram Alpha is the computational engine that not only solves the problem but shows the step-by-step work (with a subscription). Seeing how a derivative or integral is worked out is far more useful for an exam than just seeing the final number.

7. Google Calendar

Best for: not missing deadlines

Unglamorous, but the highest-ROI tool on this list. Block your study sessions like classes, set reminders two days before every due date, and color-code by course. Most academic stress comes from surprise deadlines — a calendar quietly removes them.
